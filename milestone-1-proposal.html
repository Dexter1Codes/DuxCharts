1 Abstract

Intraday traders in Indian markets act on fast-moving, noisy information. Most retail tools either hand out buy and sell signals with no reasoning or stop at raw charts. This project is an educational platform for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY and a few stocks that describes the current state of the market through trend, volatility, momentum and activity, compares the day against similar sessions from the past, and studies the Closing Auction Session that the National Stock Exchange recently introduced. Every figure comes with a plain-language explanation.

The platform is for research and study. It places no real orders, promises no returns, and gives no public buy or sell advice, which keeps it outside SEBI's Research Analyst rules. It also carries a security and data-integrity layer: role-based login, ownership checks, rate limiting, input validation, a hash of every ingested file, and an audit log of each ingestion. Because of that layer, every result on screen can be traced back to a verified source file. The sections below set out the problem, the requirements, the feasibility, and the plan for a working version by 30 November 2026.

2 Problem statement and motivation

Intraday decision-making in the Indian equity market is hard for a few recurring reasons:

  • Most retail tools show indicators or fire alerts, but rarely explain why the market looks the way it does today, so a trader cannot tell whether a signal suits current conditions.
  • Traders often ask whether they have seen a day like this one before, yet have no systematic way to find and compare similar past sessions and what followed them.
  • The Closing Auction Session changes how the closing price is set at the end of the day, and there is little accessible tooling to study how it behaves and how it relates to the regular session.

There is also the question of trusting the data. A conclusion is only as good as the file it was computed from, and most student and retail tools keep no record of what was ingested or whether it changed.

The gap this project fills

One platform that describes the state of the market, retrieves similar days from history, studies the Closing Auction Session, and (after December) forecasts short-term moves and simulates strategies, all on data whose origin can be verified, and all for learning rather than advice.

3 Objectives

The project was approved at Milestone-0 with the objectives below. The ones marked MVP are due at Milestone-2 on 30 November. The ones marked post-Dec are planned for the January to March phase before the final panel.

  • MVP  Describe the market: compute and show trend, volatility, momentum and activity for the chosen instruments.
  • MVP  Compare with history: find the past sessions most like the current one.
  • MVP  Study the Closing Auction Session and how it relates to the regular close.
  • MVP  Explain every output in plain language.
  • MVP  Protect the data and control access, with verified and audited ingestion.
  • post-Dec  Forecast short-term moves as calibrated probabilities, never as advice.
  • post-Dec  Evaluate strategies by simulating trades on historical data and measuring the result.

4 SDG alignment and rationale

The project maps most directly to SDG 4, Quality Education. It is built to teach rather than to advise. A user learns to read trend, volatility, momentum and activity, sees similar days from the past, and reads a plain explanation of each figure. Financial literacy in India is low, and most retail tools give signals without teaching anyone the reasoning, so a tool whose main output is understanding fits this goal well.

A second link is to SDG 8, Decent Work and Economic Growth, and in particular target 8.10 on access to financial services and markets. Retail investors who understand how the market behaves take part on more even terms with larger institutions, which supports fairer participation.

The work on the Closing Auction Session also touches SDG 9, target 9.5, which calls for stronger research capacity, since the session is new and little studied and the project adds careful measurement of how it behaves.

5 Background and context

The National Stock Exchange is the main venue for equity trading in India, and the NIFTY 50 and BANK NIFTY indices are its most-watched benchmarks. Retail participation has grown sharply in recent years, and intraday trading, where positions open and close within the same day, is popular among active traders. Many of them rely on technical analysis, which reads price and volume to judge trend, volatility and momentum.

The NSE recently introduced a Closing Auction Session, a short call-auction window at the end of the day in which orders are collected and a single closing price is discovered. Call auctions at the close are already used by exchanges such as the NYSE and the London Stock Exchange, because they make the closing price steadier and harder to distort, which matters for index funds and large orders that must execute at the close. Because the session is new in India, there is little tooling and little published study of how it behaves.

Market advice in India is regulated. SEBI's Research Analyst framework governs anyone who publishes buy or sell recommendations, which is why this project is built to explain and simulate rather than to advise.

7 Stakeholders and users

  • Retail intraday traders and active investors in Indian markets, the primary users, who want to understand market behaviour before they act.
  • Students and other learners building market literacy, who benefit from the explanations attached to every output.
  • The project team and the academic guide, who set and assess the work.
  • The research community interested in how the Closing Auction Session behaves, since published study of it is thin.
  • The wider aim of investor protection that SEBI's rules reflect. The no-advice design keeps the tool in line with that aim.

9 Proposed solution and methodology

The system is a pipeline in layers. The data and analysis layers run in Python and write to MongoDB. The API and the dashboard read from MongoDB and present the results. A security and integrity layer wraps the server side. The two halves are kept apart by two agreed interfaces, the MongoDB document shapes and the REST API contract, so the data work and the application work can go on in parallel.

Every protected request is checked for identity and permission, and every ingested file is hashed and logged, so each figure traces back to a verified source file.

9.1 External context and event awareness

The core analysis reads price and volume only. Markets also move on outside events such as the Union Budget, RBI policy decisions, regulatory changes, earnings and global cues, which a price-only view cannot see coming. The platform does not try to predict these events, because they are rare, hard to quantify and easy to overfit. Instead it keeps a calendar of known high-impact dates (Budget day, RBI policy dates, derivatives expiry, results dates) and flags when a session sits near one, then widens the stated uncertainty and adds a caveat, for example that volatility around RBI policy is usually higher and the model's edge is lower. This gives the user context about what is happening outside the chart. News and macro-feature analysis is left for later work.

9.2 Methodology

Development is iterative and planned around outcomes. Work is cut into pieces small enough to finish and check in a day, each with a clear definition of done: working, tested where it matters, and committed. Interfaces come first. The team settles the MongoDB document shapes and the API contract before heavy building, so the data side and the application side can move in parallel and meet in the middle. Each milestone ends in a running, demonstrable state.

11 Scope and limitations

11.1 In scope

  • Instruments: the NIFTY 50 index, the BANK NIFTY index, and a small set of liquid stocks, with the final list fixed with the guide.
  • Historical and daily end-of-day data, including Closing Auction Session data where it is available.
  • Descriptive analysis, comparison with similar past days, CAS statistics, and explained decision support.
  • A secured REST API and an interactive web dashboard.
  • Probabilistic forecasting and strategy backtesting in the phase after December.

11.2 MVP scope for 30 November 2026

  • A Python ingestion pipeline that writes cleaned data to MongoDB, with file hashing and an ingestion audit log.
  • Indicator computation (trend, volatility, momentum, activity) and a simple "days like today" comparison, with results stored in MongoDB.
  • CAS statistics over the available data.
  • A Node.js and Express REST API over MongoDB.
  • A React dashboard with four views: market overview, indicators, similar historical days, and CAS.
  • The security layer: login with roles, ownership checks, rate limiting, input validation, ingestion hashing and the audit log.

11.3 Instruments and data granularity

  • The project uses NSE instruments only: the NIFTY 50 and BANK NIFTY indices and a few liquid NSE stocks such as RELIANCE, HDFCBANK, INFY and TCS, with the final list fixed with the guide. SENSEX and other BSE instruments are left out so the Closing Auction Session analysis stays consistent, since the CAS is an NSE mechanism.
  • The pipeline treats the instrument as a data field rather than hard-coded logic, so adding indices or stocks later, for example NIFTY 150 or 500, is a matter of configuration. The validated set is kept small on purpose, and wider coverage is future work the design already allows.
  • Each stock runs through the same steps as an index: its own cleaned history, its own indicators, and a "days like today" search within its own past. Later forecasting uses one model per instrument, since each symbol behaves differently, with a pooled model as a scaling option. CAS data is recorded per security, so stock-level CAS analysis is a normal case.

11.4 Limitations

  • The December version describes and compares; it does not forecast. Forecasting arrives in the next phase.
  • CAS analysis is bounded by how much CAS data exists, which is small because the session is new.
  • The MVP uses end-of-day data, so it does not yet capture moves within the trading day. Minute-level intraday data is a later extension, once its cost and availability are confirmed.
  • The validated set is a few instruments, not the whole market.
  • Explanations are generated from templates over the computed numbers, so they are accurate but not conversational.

Regulatory guardrail

Publishing buy or sell recommendations in India falls under SEBI's Research Analyst framework. The platform explains behaviour and simulates strategies for study, and never tells a user to trade. This rule is part of the requirements (NFR-7) and shapes the design.

12 Feasibility

12.1 Technical

The stack is standard and the techniques are known. Python with pandas and NumPy handles the data and analysis, MongoDB stores it, Node.js with Express serves the API, and React builds the dashboard. The indicators (moving-average trend, range-based volatility, momentum oscillators, volume) and the similarity search behind "days like today" are standard computations. Historical price and volume data for the chosen instruments is easy to obtain. The one real unknown is how much CAS data is available, which is tracked as a risk in section 13. On balance the build is achievable.

12.2 Economic

The stack is open source and runs on the computers the team already has and on free service tiers. Data for the selected instruments costs little or nothing. The project needs no capital budget.

12.3 Operational

The work is divided by skill. Two Data Science members own the data and the analysis and write their results to MongoDB; the developer owns the API, the dashboard and the security layer. Each person works to daily deliverables with a clear finish, and the two interfaces, the MongoDB document shapes and the API contract, are agreed early so neither side waits on the other. With disciplined scope this is workable.

12.4 Schedule

The scope for 30 November is deliberately limited. Forecasting and backtesting are held back to the phase after December, which leaves the data pipeline, descriptive analysis, CAS statistics, API, dashboard and security layer for the current build. About seven weeks remain to Milestone-2, with room to absorb delays.

12.5 Legal and ethical

Because the platform teaches and never gives public buy or sell advice, it stays outside SEBI's Research Analyst registration. Data is used for study, not resold, with sources acknowledged. The educational purpose and the explanations also address the ethical risk of a tool that could otherwise be read as investment advice.

12.6 Opportunity

The Closing Auction Session is new and little studied, so even simple, careful statistics about it add something the current tools do not offer. The explainable, integrity-checked approach also carries over to other instruments and markets later, which gives the project room to grow after the capstone.

14 Validation, testing and expected outcomes

14.1 Expected outcome at Milestone-2

By 30 November the team expects a running, secured web application that describes NIFTY, BANK NIFTY and a few stocks, compares each day with similar past sessions, reports CAS statistics, and explains every figure, all over data whose origin can be verified. The forecasting and strategy-simulation work builds on this in the following phase. The MVP is judged against these criteria, all re-checkable on historical data:

  • The data pipeline ingests the chosen instruments, hashes every file, and records every ingestion in the audit log.
  • Indicators match a reference calculation within tolerance on sample dates.
  • For a given session, the system returns a ranked list of similar past days from a defined distance measure.
  • CAS statistics are computed across the sessions for which data exists.
  • The API exposes documented endpoints, enforces login and authorisation, and passes its tests.
  • The dashboard shows all four views, each with an explanation.
  • The security layer demonstrates login, roles, ownership, rate limiting and validation, and shows data integrity and the audit log end to end.

14.2 How the forecasting model is tested

The model is checked in three stages, none of which places a real trade:

  • Walk-forward backtesting. The model trains on an earlier window and is tested on the next, then the window rolls forward. The data is never shuffled, so no future information leaks into training. This is the main, repeatable test.
  • A held-out block of history the model never saw during development, scored once, for an honest estimate of real-world performance.
  • Forward, or paper, testing on the live market. The system records each day's prediction on incoming data and scores it later against what happened. This tests the model on the live market with no order ever placed.

Across all three, forecasts are judged on directional accuracy and on how well the probabilities are calibrated, for example with the Brier score and a calibration curve, compared against a simple baseline. Strategies are judged on hit rate, return against a benchmark, and largest drawdown. Results near flagged events are reported on their own, since the model is expected to do worse there. Everything is reported as research findings.
