1 Summary

The data layer of the project is now built and checked. We hold daily price history for six NSE instruments running from 2000 to the present day, and the complete published history of the Closing Auction Session, which NSE launched on 3 August 2026. Both sets have been cleaned into one schema and cross-checked against independent sources.

The strongest result is a validation. NSE's official auction reports and a separate commercial data provider agree exactly, on every one of 228 matched observations, that the auction price is the day's official closing price. That single check confirms the accuracy of both datasets, the correctness of our cleaning code, and the join key the whole application depends on.

Headline result

228 of 228 matched rows, 100%, show the CAS final price equal to the official daily close. Mean absolute difference: 0.0000.

2.3 Derived datasets

  • A per-stock panel joining each auction to that day's price and volume, with the auction's share of daily turnover and a percentile rank against the stock's own history.
  • A market-wide daily series measuring auction pressure across all 215 stocks, which stands in for the index-level view that no single auction can provide.

3 How the data was verified

We did not take any source on trust. Three checks were run.

3.1 Full-series comparison against an independent provider

An alternative dataset covering the same instruments was compared against Yahoo Finance day by day, not by sampling. For NIFTY, 4,501 of 4,502 overlapping days matched within 0.01%, a rate of 99.98%, with a median difference of 0.0000%. For BANK NIFTY the rate was 99.76%. A single day, 10 August 2015, differs by about 0.9% on both indices and is excluded from tolerance checks.

3.2 Agreement between two unrelated providers

On 3 January 2000, investing.com and the alternative dataset report identical values for NIFTY: open 1,482.15, high 1,592.90, low 1,482.15, close 1,592.20. Two providers agreeing to the paisa on a twenty-six year old session is strong evidence that neither is fabricating history.

3.3 Agreement with contemporaneous reporting

For 22 January 2026 the data gives a NIFTY close of 25,289.90. A news report published that afternoon states the index gained 132.40 points to end at 25,289.90. The figures match.

Taken together with the CAS validation in section 7, the price data behind this project can be considered accurate.

Caution carried forward

Historical stock prices differ between providers because they apply corporate action adjustments differently. RELIANCE on 3 January 2000 is 21.43 in one source and 23.10 in another. Index values agree exactly, so index history can be combined across sources, while stock history cannot. One provider per stock, for the whole series.

5 The Closing Auction Session

The closing price of a stock carries weight out of proportion to a single number. Index funds must trade at it, derivatives settle against it, and portfolios are valued on it. Until August 2026 NSE set the close as the volume-weighted average price of the final thirty minutes, which a determined participant can influence by trading in thin late-session volume.

The Closing Auction Session replaces that with a call auction. Continuous trading stops for eligible stocks. For a few minutes orders are collected without being matched. The exchange then computes the single price at which the greatest volume can execute, and every order trades at that one price. That price becomes the official close.

Pooling all end-of-day interest into one moment at one price makes the close harder to distort and easier for a large order to meet.

5.1 The terms, which are also our data fields

TermMeaning
Reference priceVolume-weighted average price between 15:00 and 15:15, the anchor the auction is measured against
Lower and upper bandHow far the auction price may move away from the reference price
Indicative equilibrium priceThe running estimate of the auction price, published live while the auction is open
Final priceThe price the auction settles at, which becomes the official close
Final volumeShares actually executed in the auction
ImbalanceQuantity left unmatched at the equilibrium price

5.2 Scope and timing

  • Launched 3 August 2026 as a twenty minute session running 3:15 to 3:35 pm.
  • Applies to Category I stocks, those with F&O contracts, currently about 215 names.
  • Indices have no auction of their own. A stock can be auctioned; an index cannot be traded directly. The index close is computed from its constituents, so the auction reaches the index indirectly.
  • From 7 December 2026 the session shortens to ten minutes, 3:30 to 3:40 pm, and expiring derivative contracts settle on the last thirty minute average instead of the auction price.

Regime break

The rule change on 7 December 2026 falls between our Milestone-2 build and the December submission. Every statistic in this report describes the launch regime, 3 August to 23 October 2026, and must be labelled as such. Figures from the two regimes cannot be pooled.

6 Normalising four CAS layouts

NSE changed the layout of the daily CAS report repeatedly during its first twelve weeks. The 57 files we hold use four different schemas.

LayoutColumnsFilesPeriodDifference
112503 Aug to 07 AugLaunch format, no bid or ask columns
2171510 Aug to 28 AugBid and ask added, mixed-case headers, CHNG and FINAL QUANTITY
3173631 Aug to 23 OctSame fields renamed to CHANGE and FINAL VOLUME, upper case
410106 Oct onlyA one-off layout with a TRADE DATE column and no auction detail

Reading these by column position would corrupt the data silently. The loader instead holds an alias table that maps every spelling NSE has used onto one canonical field name, so CHNG and CHANGE both resolve to the same column, as do FINAL QUANTITY and FINAL VOLUME. A new layout needs a new alias, not new logic.

All 57 files pass through that loader into a single table of 11,986 rows covering 215 symbols across 57 trading days, with no row dropped. This is the practical answer to the question of how differently shaped sources are brought together: one canonical schema on the inside, a small adapter for each source layout on the outside.

The same approach handles the price data, where investing.com's own column names and conventions differ from the exchange's.

7 What our CAS data shows

7.1 The validation

Joining the auction reports to the daily price data on symbol and date gives 228 matched observations for our four auction-eligible stocks. In every one of them the auction's final price equals the official closing price exactly, with a mean absolute difference of 0.0000. NSE's report and a commercial data provider, with no connection between them, agree completely. This confirms the definition of the session, the accuracy of both sources and the correctness of our loaders.

7.3 How far the auction moves price

Across all 11,986 observations the auction price sits a median of 0.25% away from the reference price and a mean of 0.35% away. Half of all auctions move price by more than 0.25%, a fifth by more than 0.5%, and 4.3% by more than 1%. The largest deviation observed is exactly 3.0000%, which suggests a band limit rather than a market outcome and is worth confirming against the circular.

7.4 A persistent upward bias

Across the whole sample 60.0% of auctions close above their reference price and 37.8% below, with the remainder unchanged. The mean signed deviation is +0.107%. On individual days the share closing above reference runs as high as 88%. Daily auction turnover across the 215 stocks ranges from about 1,270 to 2,240 crore rupees.

Original observation

The closing auction appears to carry a systematic upward bias in its first twelve weeks. We have not found this reported elsewhere. It is a descriptive finding over a short sample and is offered as an observation to investigate, not a conclusion.

7.5 Fields NSE publishes but does not fill

The indicative equilibrium quantity and both imbalance columns are present in the file headers and empty in every row we hold. The indicative equilibrium price is populated in only 8.7% of rows, almost all from the earliest files. Any analysis depending on imbalance is therefore not currently possible.

8 The working model

The December system trains no model. Its analytical core is a similarity search, which is deterministic and can be re-run to the same answer by an examiner. It works in five steps.

  • Every past trading day is reduced to a vector of its indicator values, covering trend, volatility, momentum and activity. This is the day's fingerprint.
  • Each feature is standardised so that volume, measured in millions, cannot overwhelm a momentum reading on a scale of nought to a hundred.
  • The distance between today's vector and every historical day's vector is measured.
  • The closest days are taken and ranked, which is nearest-neighbour retrieval.
  • What happened on the session after each of those days is summarised as a distribution, for example that thirteen of the twenty closest days closed higher the next session.

The result is presented as historical precedent. The system reports what has happened before under similar conditions and never advises an action, which keeps it clear of SEBI's Research Analyst framework.

Forecasting belongs to the phase after December. It will use a supervised classifier producing calibrated probabilities, validated by walk-forward testing on history and then by recording predictions on live data without placing any trade. No neural network is involved, because the sample is small and every output has to be explainable.

9 What the December version delivers

A secured web application in which a user logs in and, for the six tracked instruments, can do the following.

  • See the current state of an instrument, covering trend, volatility, momentum and activity, with a plain-language reading.
  • Inspect the indicators behind that reading, each explained.
  • Retrieve historically similar sessions and see what followed them.
  • Examine the closing auction for the four auction-eligible stocks, and a market-wide auction pressure view built from all 215.
  • Keep a personal watchlist visible only to that account.

The system places no orders, connects to no broker and issues no recommendation.

10.3 Security and integrity

Every protected route requires authentication and checks the caller's role, and anything belonging to a user is additionally checked for ownership. Inputs are validated and traffic is rate limited. On the data side, every ingested file is hashed and recorded before it is read, so any figure on screen can be traced back to a known file. The quality report already produced for the daily data carries the source hash and is the first working piece of that chain.
